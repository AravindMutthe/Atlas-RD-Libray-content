## Research ontology and cross-links (original recovered context)

1. **Communications:** couriers/semaphore → electrical telegraph → telephone/broadcast → computer packet networks → TCP/IP Internet → mobile/cloud/edge → AI-assisted networks.
2. **Network engineering:** media, encoding, addressing, switching/routing, protocols, devices, ownership, operations, regulation, security, storage, pricing and user interfaces per era.
3. **Institutional/business systems:** contracts, trade, banks, railways, ledgers, enterprise software, procurement, regulatory oversight and global supply chains.
4. **Operating systems:** serial TTY, NIC drivers, kernel network stack, namespaces, routing, netfilter, IPC, schedulers, memory and storage, with analogy limits clearly stated.
5. **Architecture:** monolithic vs modular vs distributed vs service-oriented and programmable networking are independent architectural choices, not a one-way historical succession.
6. **AI:** distinguish AI traffic over ordinary networks, AI-managed network operations and communication among AI agents; retain human approval and auditing.

## Evidence and accuracy safeguards

- A prior assistant's historical claim is a record of that answer, not independent verification of the claim.
- ARPA, DARPA and ARPANET must be distinguished; ARPANET was a research packet network, not synonymous with the entire Internet.
- The assertion that the telegraph was the 'first business network' is an oversimplification; older courier, postal, maritime and merchant networks predate it.
- Linux source paths and network feature support are version/configuration-dependent.
- Historical analogies such as business departments ≈ VLANs or government branches ≈ kernel subsystems are pedagogical metaphors, not structural identities.
