## Key cross-era research questions
1. What was the communication medium, addressing method, switching/routing model, and physical device in each era?
2. Who owned, funded, regulated, operated and consumed the network? How were access, contracts and tariffs negotiated?
3. Which functions were monolithic, modular, interoperable, virtualized, programmable or AI-assisted? These are architecture choices, not exclusive historical epochs.
4. How did interfaces, protocols, identities, storage, operating systems, hardware, semiconductor supply chains and software evolve together?
5. How do historical ideas map to modern Linux/kernel networking and distributed systems without equating analogy with mechanism?
6. Which functions can AI automate, and which require human authorization, governance, audit and control?

## Terminology corrections
- DARPA is the Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency; ARPA was its earlier name. ARPANET was a research packet-switched network, not identical to today's Internet.
- Monolithic versus modular is a design distinction seen in many eras, not a simple telegraph-to-AI chronological replacement.
- 'AI network' is not a single universally defined protocol family; distinguish AI workloads over networks, AI-assisted network operations, and interconnections among AI services/agents.
- Linux kernel source-path references are illustrations and can vary by version and configuration.
